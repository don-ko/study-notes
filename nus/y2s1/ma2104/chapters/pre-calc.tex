\section{``Pre-Calculus''}
\paragraph{Lines}
Given a point $S$, its \textcolor{Bittersweet}{distance to the line} L is given by:
$\frac{\left|\vv{PS}\times \mathbf{v}\right|}{\left| \mathbf{v} \right| }$

\paragraph{Planes}
To find the \textcolor{Blue}{distance from a point} from a point to a plane,
find a vector from the plane to the point $\mathbf{v}$, and take the dot
product of $\mathbf{v}$ with $\mathbf{n}$ to find the scalar multiple of
$\mathbf{n}$.
\[\text{Distance}=\left| \frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot \mathbf{v}}{\left| \mathbf{v} \right| } \right|.\] 

\paragraph{Cylinders}
Given a curve $z=f(y)$ on the $yz$-plane, ``moved'' along the $x$-axis, we have
points $\left( x,y,f(y) \right) $ on the \textcolor{Bittersweet}{cylinder generated by
the curve}, where $x$ is arbitrary. The surface has the equation $z=f(y)$. The
other planes and axes can also be used.
